Hungarian Algorithm & Real-World Use Cases

1. Why pad rectangular matrices? (Square Matrix requirement)

The classical Hungarian Algorithm strictly requires a square cost matrix ($N \times N$) where rows (agents/workers) equal columns (tasks/jobs). If rectangular (e.g., 3 workers and 5 tasks), we pad the matrix with dummy rows or columns filled with zeros so every row pairs with a unique column. Dummy assignments in the final output are ignored.

2. Where is this algorithm used in the real world?

  • Task & Job Assignment: Assigning $N$ employees to $N$ projects to minimize total completion time or cost.
  • Ride-Sharing & Delivery Dispatch (Uber/DoorDash): Matching nearby drivers to pickup requests simultaneously to minimize ETA.
  • Computer Vision & Tracking (MOT): Matching bounding boxes from frame $t$ to frame $t+1$ based on minimum distance.
  • Cloud Computing: Distributing microservices across computing nodes to minimize network latency.
